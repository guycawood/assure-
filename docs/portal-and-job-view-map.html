Portal and Job View Map

How System Guy is organised for the people who use it: one job system that carries a job from brief to cash, and six internal portals that look over the parts of that job. This page sets what a buyer sees, what stays inside a division, and who gets access to what. Items marked to confirm are my assumptions.

Three pillars

Client

Gives the brief, then mainly receives status and reporting. Approves what needs a decision.

  • Client portal
  • Approvers decide, viewers look

Internal team

Buyers move jobs from brief to cash. Divisions step in along the way and manage their own area.

  • Job system for buyers
  • Six internal portals for divisions

Vendor

Onboards once through SRM, keeps the profile current, is invited to RFQs, prices, wins, produces and gets paid.

  • Vendor portal
  • Mostly SRM

Two layers inside the internal team

Job system (the buyer's view)

  • One workspace per job, from brief to cash.
  • Shows each stage, which division owns it, its status, what blocks it and the buyer's next action.
  • Shows only the outcome of a division's step. The buyer opens a portal screen only when they must act.
  • A buyer works for one client. Several buyers can share a client.

Portals (the division's view)

  • Each division manages its own area: queue, workload, exceptions, approvals.
  • Divisional owners and their teams see all clients and all jobs in their division.
  • Buyers don't see how a division runs. They engage with it as the job moves.
  • Each portal has operators (do the work) and managers (oversee it).

Each division's Watchtower

The Watchtower is where a division governs itself. It is separate from the portal screens that operators use, and buyers never see it.

What a division manages there

  • People: its own users and role hierarchy, and what each level can see and do.
  • Libraries: its governed reference lists, versioned and audited.
  • Methodologies: how it scores, grades and decides, for example the scorecard and Impact Grade methodology.
  • Taxonomies: its classifications, for example product taxonomy and categories.
  • Change requests and audit: every change recorded, with a second person approving where it matters.

The Watchtower already has libraries, change requests and audit, and the product taxonomy. Division-level user hierarchies are not built as described here. to confirm

Strategic tools for Sourcing and SRM

  • Scenarios: run what-ifs across the whole spend and supplier base, for example moving volume between vendors, markets or rate cards, or changing a tolerance band.
  • Strategic sourcing: category strategy, spend analysis, consolidation and savings opportunities across all clients.
  • Strategic vendor management: segment vendors, plan development, concentration and risk, and review performance across the whole base.
  • Available to the senior levels of each division, across all clients.

Not built as scenario tools. Parts exist to build on: the balanced scorecard, spend and savings reports, supplier scores, and the strategic route on onboarding. to confirm What scenarios must answer first.

The vendor portal

The vendor's own portal is organised around the vendor, not around any one client. One vendor company can hold jobs from several clients, depending on which buyer chooses them. They see all of their own jobs in one place, and they run their side of each job there.

What a vendor manages

  • Profile: company details, team, sites, capabilities and tooling, subscription.
  • Compliance: due diligence, certificates and documents, contracts, expiry dates.
  • Jobs: quote requests, pushed prices, creative and sample jobs, orders.
  • Production: proofs, technical and packaging data, shipping and proof of delivery, installations and recces.
  • Quality: QA/QC plans and checkpoints, non-conformances, action plans.
  • Money: invoices and payments.
  • Relationship: performance and issues, business reviews, messages, surveys, training.

Who sees the vendor

  • The vendor: only their own company's profile and jobs.
  • Buyers: eligibility, score and the job they chose the vendor for. Not the vendor's diligence evidence.
  • SRM owners (procurement and supply chain): every vendor and every job, filterable by vendor, client, region, market and status. They also see vendor payments: where each vendor is on the way to being paid, when they will be, and whether they have been.
  • Clients: status of their own work only. Not the vendor's other clients.

The vendor sees the client's name on each job as a reference, so they can tell their jobs apart across clients. Most of this portal exists today.

Each portal's admin panel shows everything coming through that division, across all clients, so owners and teams can run the business. The same filters apply to its reporting. Buyers do not get this view. They see their own client's jobs in the job workspace.
